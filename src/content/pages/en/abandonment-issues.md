## Does this sound familiar?

You notice small shifts in tone, timing and attention. A late reply becomes evidence. You might hold on tightly, or pull away first so it cannot happen to you. Underneath is often an old conviction: that you will eventually be left.

Abandonment sensitivity is not neediness. It is a nervous system that learned to scan for signs of loss because, at some point, that scanning was useful.

## Where it comes from

Early experiences of inconsistency — a parent who was present one day and absent the next, a sudden move, a loss that was never discussed — can leave a child with the impression that relationships are fragile. As an adult, the vigilance continues even when the present situation is safe.

## How therapy can help

We work with the alarm itself: the moment the body reacts before the mind has caught up. Hypnotherapy helps settle that response and install a more accurate inner reference point. Regression can trace where the expectation began, so it can be recognised as history rather than truth.

In practical terms, we also look at what you do when the fear rises — the text you send, the silence you withdraw into — and create alternatives that feel manageable.

## First step

You do not have to arrive with a clear story. We can start with what happens in your body when someone goes quiet.
