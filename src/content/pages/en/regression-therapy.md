## Why go back?

Regression therapy works on the assumption that present reactions often carry the imprint of earlier experience. A sudden panic, a repeated relationship dynamic, a bodily tension with no obvious cause — these can lead back to a moment that has not fully been digested.

In a session we follow what arises, at your pace. That may be a childhood memory, a significant loss, or a period of life you have not thought about in years. You are never pushed into material; you can pause, open your eyes or stop at any point.

## What it can help with

- abandonment and fear of commitment
- grief that has not settled
- recurring emotional patterns
- unexplained fears and physical tension
- questions about identity and direction

## What a session looks like

We start with your intention for the session, then use deep relaxation to access the material that wants attention. What follows is a careful, curious exploration rather than a reliving. Afterwards we take time to integrate what came up and connect it to your present life.

Sessions last ninety minutes. Some people need one; others come for a short series of three or four. We evaluate together, and there is no expectation to continue longer than is useful.

## Safety and boundaries

Regression can touch strong material. That is why the pace is slow, the room is quiet, and you remain in control throughout. If a memory feels too large to approach, we work around it or leave it for another time. This method sits alongside — never instead of — medical or psychological treatment where that is needed.
