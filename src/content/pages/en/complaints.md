## If something is not right

Care is a collaboration, and sometimes expectations do not match. Perhaps communication did not feel right, an approach did not suit you, or you were left with an unanswered question. You are always welcome to say so.

Raising a concern will not affect the care you receive, and it will not be held against you.

## Informal route

The first step is usually a conversation with Edward. You can mention it at the start of a session, send an email or ask for a separate call. The aim is to understand what happened and to find a solution together.

If you prefer not to speak directly, you may put your concern in writing. You will receive a response within two weeks.

## Formal complaint

If a conversation does not resolve the matter, you can submit a written complaint to the practice. Include:

- your name and contact details
- what happened, and when
- what outcome you are seeking

You will receive confirmation of receipt within five working days and a substantive response within four weeks. If more time is needed, you will be informed and given a new date.

## Independent dispute resolution

If the complaint cannot be resolved satisfactorily, it can be submitted to the independent dispute committee affiliated with the professional association of which the practice is a member. The committee's binding opinion is available on request, and details are provided at the start of a trajectory.

## Privacy complaints

Concerns about the handling of personal data are covered by the privacy policy. You may raise them with the practice or file a complaint with the Dutch Data Protection Authority (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens).

## Record keeping

A complaint and its handling are recorded confidentially and kept for the period required by law. The record is separate from your treatment file and does not affect the care itself.
