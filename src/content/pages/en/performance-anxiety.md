## The stakes feel too high

Your mind goes blank. Your voice tightens. Your hands shake, your face flushes, and the audience — a room, a panel, a single examiner — becomes the only thing in the world.

Afterwards you replay it, certain that everyone noticed. The anticipation before the next time starts earlier, and the avoidance grows.

## The body speaks first

Performance anxiety is a survival response aimed at the wrong target. The body prepares for physical threat — faster heart, shallow breath, diverted blood flow — while what you actually need is access to memory, language and presence.

Trying to calm down by force usually backfires. The system reads the effort as further evidence of danger.

## How hypnotherapy helps

In a relaxed, focused state we:

- rehearse the situation in detail, including the parts you fear
- train a slower physiological response you can return to on the day
- replace catastrophic images with realistic, usable ones
- build a short pre-performance routine that anchors calm

Between sessions you practise briefly and often. The aim is not to eliminate nerves — some arousal helps — but to keep them from taking over.

## Who this is for

Speakers, musicians, athletes, students, professionals in interviews or assessments, and anyone whose work requires being observed. The method adapts to the setting.

## First step

Bring the specific moment you are dreading. Detail makes the rehearsal useful.
