## Grief does not follow a schedule

There is a cultural story that grief moves through fixed stages and then ends. In reality it arrives in waves, goes quiet for months, and returns on an ordinary Tuesday in the supermarket. Anniversaries, songs and smells can reopen what felt settled.

Grief is not a problem to be solved. But it can become stuck, and then it needs company.

## When grief becomes stuck

You may recognise one of these:

- a loss you were never allowed to mourn openly
- grief that arrived alongside practical demands, so you kept going
- a relationship with the person who died that was complicated
- a life change — illness, divorce, leaving a country — that has not been acknowledged as loss

In these situations, the feeling does not disappear. It waits.

## How therapy can help

We start by making room: for the story, the details, the parts that are difficult to say. Hypnotherapy can help regulate the intensity so that grief can be felt without flooding. Regression work can visit a memory that has not been integrated and give it a different place in your history.

Adjustment difficulties — after a move, diagnosis or role change — are approached in the same way: what has been lost, what is still here, and what needs time.

## First step

You do not have to tell the whole story in a first conversation. Bring whatever is closest.
