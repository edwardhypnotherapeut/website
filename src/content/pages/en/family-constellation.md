## The wider picture

A family constellation is a guided group process. Participants place representatives — people from the group — in space to represent family members or elements of a question. What unfolds often reveals loyalties, entanglements and unspoken dynamics that individual conversation cannot reach.

You do not need to believe in anything for it to be useful. The work is experiential: you observe, feel, and notice what shifts. Many people describe a sudden clarity about a pattern that had felt inexplicable.

## What it can help with

- recurring family patterns
- grief, estrangement or cut-off relationships
- feelings of guilt or over-responsibility
- questions about belonging
- transitions and inherited burdens

## What a session looks like

We meet in a small group, usually six to ten people. After a short round of introductions, one participant sets up their constellation with my guidance. Everyone is welcome to observe, and taking a role is always voluntary.

Group sessions run for two hours. Individual constellation work is also possible within a therapy session, using objects or floor markers instead of representatives. It is a quieter form, but often surprisingly precise.

## What it is not

A constellation is not a diagnosis, a verdict or a replacement for therapy. It offers an image, not an answer. The value lies in what you do with that image in the weeks afterwards — which is why we always take time to reflect and ground at the end.
