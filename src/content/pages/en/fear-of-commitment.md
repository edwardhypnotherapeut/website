## The push and pull

You want a relationship, and then you find reasons to keep it at arm's length. Someone gets close and you notice a sudden need for space, a flaw you cannot unsee, or a restlessness that makes staying feel impossible.

At the same time, the distance does not bring the relief you hoped for. It brings doubt, loneliness, or the quiet suspicion that you are the problem.

## What sits underneath

Fear of commitment is often a protective strategy rather than a lack of feeling. It can grow from watching a difficult relationship as a child, from being told that closeness costs freedom, or from an earlier experience in which losing yourself in someone else went badly.

The nervous system then treats commitment as a threat — not because it is, but because it once was.

## How therapy can help

We make the pattern conscious: what triggers the urge to leave, what it protects you from, and what you would need in order to stay. Hypnotherapy helps lower the alarm around closeness; regression can reveal where the association between love and loss began.

The goal is not to force a decision. It is to make sure your decisions come from choice rather than fear.
