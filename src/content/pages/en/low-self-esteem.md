## The inner critic is not the truth

It sounds like your own voice, which is what makes it convincing. You hear it before a meeting, after a conversation, in the mirror. It speaks in absolutes: always, never, everyone, stupid, too much.

Low self-esteem is not a lack of achievements. Many people with a harsh inner critic are competent, liked and outwardly confident. The gap between how they appear and how they feel is part of the strain.

## Where the voice comes from

The critic is usually learned. It may have started as a way to stay ahead of criticism from someone else, or as an attempt to be good enough in an environment where approval was conditional. It once had a protective job. It has simply never been told that the job is done.

## What we work on

- recognising the critic as a voice rather than a fact
- tolerating praise and attention without deflecting
- setting boundaries without a wave of guilt
- replacing comparison with a more accurate self-image

## How therapy helps

In a relaxed state, the critic is easier to hear — and easier to talk back to. We test the beliefs underneath it, install more realistic internal language, and rehearse situations where you would normally shrink. Regression work can reveal the moment the voice first appeared, which often loosens its authority.

## First step

You do not need to arrive believing in yourself. Starting is enough.
