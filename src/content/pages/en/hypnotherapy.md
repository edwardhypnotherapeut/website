## What hypnotherapy is — and is not

Hypnotherapy is a guided state of focused attention. You remain awake and aware throughout; nothing is done to you, and no one can make you say or do anything against your will. In practice it feels like a deep, pleasant concentration — similar to being absorbed in a book, or to the moment just before sleep.

In that state, the usual critical chatter quietens a little. That makes it easier to work with beliefs, habits and emotional reactions that have resisted ordinary effort.

## What it can help with

Hypnotherapy is used for a wide range of concerns, including:

- anxiety and social anxiety
- sleep problems and insomnia
- procrastination and avoidance
- low confidence and self-esteem
- performance anxiety
- unhelpful habits and emotional patterns

It is not a cure-all, and it is not a replacement for medical or psychiatric care. If something falls outside my scope, I will say so and help you find the right support.

## What a session looks like

We begin with a short conversation about what has changed since last time and what you want from today. Then you settle into a comfortable chair — or your own space, if we meet online — and I guide you into a relaxed, focused state. Most of the work happens through imagery, suggestion and gentle questioning. You leave with a few practical ideas to carry into the week.

The first session is seventy-five minutes; follow-up sessions are usually sixty. Most people notice something after one or two sessions, but we plan for the longer arc so the change has time to settle.

## A note on expectations

Hypnotherapy is not magic, and it is not passive. Its effect depends on your involvement, on the fit between us, and on what the material asks for. I would rather promise a careful process than a miracle. If progress stalls, we talk about it openly and change direction.
